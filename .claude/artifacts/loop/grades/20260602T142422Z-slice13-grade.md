## Decisions

### DEC-004: ESLint-disable per function rather than threshold raise

**Rationale**: 3 functions hit complexity 19-23 vs threshold 15. Considered raising the global threshold to 20. Rejected — would reduce signal for genuinely tangled growth across the codebase. Scoped suppressions tagged for extraction follow-up keep the bar honest.

**Consequences**:
- positive: global complexity threshold stays as a sharp signal
- negative: 3 outstanding suppressions need future extraction work
- negative: complexity wallpaper risk if the pattern spreads

## Surprises

- SLICE-13's own ceremony emitted both `cost-report-slice-` AND `cost-report-aggregate-` variants — FEAT-034 dogfooded itself via the slice-close ceremony's cost-report write.

## What went well

End-to-end honesty fix: per-slice costHealth signal now reflects real per-session reread count (~9) rather than aggregated multi-worktree noise (~114). The arc's "F" grade was aggregation noise, not regression. New `cost-report-slice-` files auto-emit on slice-complete.

## What went wrong

Builder hit context ceiling on the meatiest slice of the arc (largest file diff: cost-advisor.mjs 865 lines + briefing/collect.mjs 777 lines). Lead recovery worked but was higher-cost than dispatching a fresh builder would have been.

## What I would do differently next time

Pre-decompose large-file slices into 2 dispatches: emission-side (artifacts.mjs + crew.mjs) and read-side (briefing/collect.mjs + briefing.mjs + tests). Builders cope better with single-domain scope than cross-domain.
